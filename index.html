What Factors Influence College Graduation Rates?
Graduation rates vary widely across universities. In this interactive article, we explore three possible factors: institution type (public vs private), tuition, and student-faculty ratio. Scroll to compare patterns and see which factors seem most related to graduation outcomes.
Do Public or Private Universities Graduate More Students?
We start by comparing average graduation rates between public and private institutions. Use the region filter on the right to explore whether this gap holds across different parts of the country.
Looking Closer at Individual Colleges
Each dot now represents one college within its bar. While private institutions average higher rates overall, there is considerable overlap — many public universities outperform private peers in the same region.
Does Higher Tuition Improve Graduation Rates?
Next, we look at the relationship between tuition cost and graduation rate. If tuition increases, do graduation rates increase too or does the pattern vary by school type? The chart on the right shows every college as a dot; blue dots are public institutions and orange dots are private. Use the filters on the right to limit the data by institution type or maximum tuition.
Interpreting the Pattern
The scatter plot suggests that higher-cost schools tend to have higher graduation rates, although there is considerable overlap. Public and private schools follow similar upward trends, but private institutions make up most of the high-tuition, high-completion points.
Do Smaller Classes Matter More Than Tuition?
Here we explore whether student-faculty ratio (a proxy for class size / attention) relates to graduation outcomes. Do schools with more faculty per student show higher graduation rates?
What This Suggests
The chart shows a small positive relationship between student-faculty ratio and graduation rate, but the pattern is not strong. Schools with more full-time faculty do not always have much higher completion rates.

Private colleges tend to have higher graduation rates than public colleges even at similar faculty ratios, which suggests that class size alone cannot explain student success.

Overall, smaller classes may help, but graduation outcomes are likely influenced by many other factors such as student preparation, financial support, and campus resources.
Does Selectivity Predict Graduation?
Finally, we look at admission rate as a proxy for student preparation and institutional selectivity. Hover over any dot to explore individual schools. More selective schools (lower admission rates) tend to show substantially higher graduation rates.
Conclusion: How Do Regions Compare?
To wrap up, we look at average graduation rates by geographic region. Regional differences reflect the combined effect of institution mix, tuition levels, faculty investment, and student selectivity.
